Prompt: If two people can't agree and both trust themselves more, who wins?

Claude's Response:
Usually whoever has more power in that specific context—not whoever's actually right.

That's the uncomfortable part. When two people each trust their own judgment and can't agree, "who wins" often comes down to things unrelated to correctness:

- **Authority**: The boss, the parent, the person with the title.
- **Leverage**: Who can walk away, who controls resources, who needs the other less.
- **Persistence**: Who's willing to keep fighting longest.
- **Social backing**: Who can rally more people to their side.

None of those track truth reliably.

A few honest observations:

1. **"Trusting yourself more" is the default for almost everyone.** It's not evidence you're right. The other person feels exactly as certain as you do, with the same conviction.

2. **If winning is the goal, the relationship is usually the loser.** Disagreements settled by force or status tend to leave resentment, even when the "winner" was correct.

3. **The useful move is to stop asking "who's right" and ask "how would we know?"** Is there a testable prediction? A neutral standard? A way to try one approach and check the result? That converts a standoff into something solvable.

4. **Sometimes the right answer is no resolution**—just deciding it doesn't matter enough to fight over, or going separate ways on it.

What's the actual situation? The general answer is bleak, but the specifics usually have better options than "someone wins."
